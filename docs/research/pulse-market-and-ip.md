# Seatfold Pulse: market, gaps and IP protection

Research date: 11 November 2026. Scope: who already sells cinema demand forecasting, pricing, programming and analytics; what independent and small-chain UK operators cannot get; how a small company can protect Pulse; and whether a cross-operator data pool is lawful under UK GDPR.

How to read this document:

- Every claim has a URL. "Verified" means we opened that page during this research. "Search only" means the statement comes from a search summary or a page we could not open (paywall, 403 or 405), and should be checked before anyone relies on it.
- Vendor figures (uplifts, savings, accuracy) are the vendor's own and are not independently audited unless stated.
- This is research, not legal advice. Before filing anything or signing a data-pooling contract, get a short written opinion from a patent attorney or a data protection solicitor.

## 1. Summary

1. **The forecasting and AI tools that exist are enterprise products, sold bundled with a ticketing system or as a consultancy-style rollout.** Vista has machine-learning admission forecasts (Cinema Intelligence), assisted scheduling and AI dashboards, aimed at circuits. Showtime Analytics (with Admit One under Showtime Group Solutions) sells analytics, Gower Street forecasts and "agentic AI" to chains. Smart Pricer sells dynamic pricing to chains through A/B-tested rollouts. Veezi, Vista's product for independents, lists reporting but no forecasting or AI.
2. **Nobody we found publishes per-show forecast accuracy.** The only accuracy-type figure is a second-hand report that a Vista tool predicts opening weekends within plus or minus 15%. Everything else is uplift or time-saving claims.
3. **Independent cinemas are building their own tools with general chatbots.** Screen Daily (April 2026) reported a two-screen Norwegian cinema running its own scheduling and marketing agents on Claude, and the Czech cinema association saying more than half of Czech independents use AI in some form. That is the gap Pulse fills: explainable, per-show numbers built into the booking system, at an independent's budget.
4. **Payroll pressure makes staffing forecasts more valuable than pricing for UK independents.** The UK Cinema Association estimated the April 2025 Living Wage and National Insurance changes add 6% to 12% to cinema payroll. Dynamic pricing carries reputational risk (AMC's Sightline was dropped in 2023; a Village Cinemas surge-pricing trial was dropped after backlash).
5. **Patents are not a realistic first move.** The Supreme Court in Emotional Perception ([2026] UKSC 3, 11 February 2026) dropped the Aerotel test, so a claim that uses a computer now clears the first hurdle. But features that are not technical (forecasting demand, setting prices, business rules) are filtered out before inventive step. The EPO case the Supreme Court relied on, Duns Licensing (T 154/04), was itself about estimating sales and was held to be a non-technical business research method. Emotional Perception's own application was refused again on 1 October 2026 for lack of inventive step.
6. **Trade secrets are the natural protection for Pulse, but the current position undermines them.** The repository `Git-Dann/cinema-white-label` is public on GitHub (checked via the GitHub API on 11 November 2026), and `docs/pulse.md` publishes the full method with constants (k = 150, w = F(h)^0.7, the pricing thresholds and so on) on the pushed `claude/pulse` branch. Information that is "readily accessible" is not a trade secret under the Trade Secrets (Enforcement, etc.) Regulations 2018, and public disclosure also counts against novelty for any later patent. This is the single most urgent action item.
7. **Cross-operator learning exists in cinema, but only at box office level or for advertising.** Rentrak (formerly Comscore Movies, sold to Advaya Capital in 2026) pools transaction-level grosses from about 34,000 theatres. Movio's Madex service combines exhibitor data for distributor advertising, with Movio acting as processor for most of it and as an independent controller on legitimate interests for one part. In the arts, TRG Arts / Purple Seven run an anonymised benchmark across 400+ organisations. We found no one pooling per-show demand patterns across independent cinemas to improve each operator's forecasts.
8. **The UK GDPR position on a data pool is workable but changes our role.** The ICO says a processor that uses client data for its own purposes (including training its own model) becomes a controller for that processing. Pooling only show-level counts (admissions per screening, no customer identifiers, small counts suppressed) keeps the pool outside personal data in practice, but producing those aggregates must be a documented, instructed processing step in our contracts. Competition law also matters: a platform that shares competitors' pricing or forward-looking data can act as a "hub".

### Notes on individual competitors

**Vista Group is the main threat at the top of the market.** It owns Cinema Intelligence outright (50% in 2016, the rest later, date not confirmed; [GlobeNewswire 2015](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/12/22/1146203/0/en/Vista-Group-International-to-Acquire-Stake-in-Share-Dimension-the-Makers-of-Cinema-Intelligence.html), search only). Cinema Intelligence is described as "film forecasting, distribution negotiation, automated scheduling, and business analysis" and helps "optimise forecasting, booking decisions, and staff scheduling using historical and projected data" ([Vista Classic page](https://www.vista.co/vista-classic), verified). The Flix Brewhouse case study (seven sites) is the clearest public evidence of forecast-to-staffing: a 36-day trial cut 924 over-scheduled payroll hours, projected at about USD 70,000 a year per cinema; no forecast accuracy is reported ([case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/vista/case-study/flix-brewhouse-customer-case-study), verified). Vista's newest pricing work (April 2026) is a rules engine for price cards, not demand-based pricing ([Vista](https://vista.co/insights/redefining-ticket-pricing), verified). None of this reaches Veezi, Vista's product for independents ([veezi.com](https://www.veezi.com/), verified).

**Showtime is the closest to Pulse in ambition.** Its homepage now leads with an "AI Access Layer" that lets operators ask questions in plain language, including through ChatGPT, and agents that "monitor performance, trigger actions, and optimise decisions in real time" ([showtimeanalytics.com](https://showtimeanalytics.com/), verified). It gives no forecasting method or accuracy, and it sells to chains first. It says it serves "single independent sites and enterprise chains" ([Showtime platform](https://www.showtimeanalytics.com/home), verified).

**Pricing vendors are chain-scale.** Smart Pricer's rollout model (test sites against control sites, a pricing team or POS integration) suits circuits ([Smart Pricer](https://www.smart-pricer.com/industries/cinema/), verified). Spektrix leaves automation to partners and offers free spreadsheets and reports ([Spektrix](https://www.spektrix.com/en-gb/dynamic-pricing-solutions), verified).

**AI and LLM cinema assistants marketed in 2024 to 2026:** Showtime "AI Access Layer" and agents (above); Vista natural-language dashboards, Oneview daily AI podcast, Movio "First Draft" copywriting ([VistaCon26](https://vista.co/insights/vistacon26-illuminating-our-future), [Celluloid Junkie](https://celluloidjunkie.com/2024/12/06/you-cant-spell-vista-group-without-ai/), both verified); CINEsync AI support assistant ([press release](https://www.issuewire.com/cinesync-revolutionises-global-cinema-operations-with-ai-driven-revenue-intelligence-1855771974469256), verified, sponsored); Filmgrail AI personalisation (search only). Plus DIY: Tynset Kino's own Claude-based scheduling and marketing agents ([Asia Business Daily citing Screen Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026042001274562353), verified).

## 3. Gaps: what independents and small chains cannot get today

What we can show from sources:

1. **Per-show forecasts at an independent price point.** Vista's forecasting sits in its enterprise tier ([Vista Classic page](https://www.vista.co/vista-classic)); Veezi has none ([veezi.com](https://www.veezi.com/)); Gower Street is territory-level and aimed at distributors ([FORECAST](https://gower.st/forecast/)); Showtime and Smart Pricer quote on request and sell to chains. All verified.
2. **Forecasts you can check.** No vendor publishes per-show error figures. Pulse can differentiate by showing its own backtest error per operator and an 80% range on every number.
3. **Explanations.** Vendors describe outcomes ("AI surfaces insights") not reasons. Pulse's per-forecast drivers ("Week 3 of release: ×0.49") are unusual in this market. We found no competitor that shows why a forecast is what it is.
4. **Staffing from forecasts.** Only the Vista/MovieTeam/Cinema Intelligence combination shows it publicly, for a US chain ([case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/vista/case-study/flix-brewhouse-customer-case-study), verified). For UK operators it matters more after April 2025: the UKCA's Phil Clapp estimated payroll up "somewhere between 6% and 12%", and Watershed in Bristol expected £199,000 extra ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/uk-exhibitors-sound-alarm-over-incoming-tax-increases-and-wage-rises/5199010.article), search only).
5. **Small cinemas are already improvising with chatbots.** Dan Kratky of the Czech cinema association said many small cinemas that cannot afford large BI and ticketing systems are turning to general AI tools; Tynset Kino reports a day a week saved and admissions up 21% (Screen Daily via [Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026042001274562353), verified; the Screen Daily original returned 405 to our tools: [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/features/how-major-and-indie-cinema-chains-are-using-ai-tools-they-are-saving-me-at-least-one-day-a-week/5215694.article)). A warning in the same piece: "AI still hallucinates a lot" (search only). Pulse's deterministic numbers with optional AI on top answer that directly.
6. **Market context for small screens.** Rentrak's Lucy Jones told the UKCA 2026 conference that keeping saturation releases on screen is hard "especially for cinemas with fewer screens" ([Celluloid Junkie](https://celluloidjunkie.com/2026/06/22/ukca-2026-conference-box-office-optimism-engaging-gen-z-and-leveraging-the-power-of-community/), verified). That supports screen-swap and programming suggestions.
7. **Financial fragility.** Independent Cinema Office surveys: 45% of 157 venues forecast a loss in 2023 and 61% were missing income targets; top priorities were increasing audiences (87%) and revenue (70%) ([ICO report page](https://www.independentcinemaoffice.org.uk/advice-support/the-financial-health-of-the-independent-theatrical-film-exhibition-sector/), verified). The November 2024 follow-up reported nearly half in deficit (search only, [Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/en/newsdetail/451801)). Price sensitivity is high, so tools must be cheap and show money saved.
8. **Pricing is delicate.** UKCA conference participants said the problem is "perception of value for money", not price cuts ([Celluloid Junkie](https://celluloidjunkie.com/2026/06/22/ukca-2026-conference-box-office-optimism-engaging-gen-z-and-leveraging-the-power-of-community/), verified). AMC dropped Sightline ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/amc-theatres-movie-ticket-prices-pilot-program-canceled-2023-summer-movies/13527503/), search only) and Village Cinemas dropped a surge trial after backlash (search only, via [Screen Daily](https://screendaily.com/features/will-cinemas-embrace-variable-pricing/5169725.article)). Pulse's operator-approved, discount-led suggestions fit this; marketing should lead with filling quiet shows, not surge pricing.

What we could not show: direct operator quotes asking for forecasting software. UK forums and ICO reports talk about audiences, funding and costs, not tools. Treat "operators want forecasting" as a hypothesis to test in customer interviews (see open questions).

## 4. Protecting Pulse: IP options

### 4.1 Patents

- **Law now.** The Supreme Court in *Emotional Perception AI Ltd v Comptroller General* [2026] UKSC 3 (11 February 2026) unanimously dropped the Aerotel test and adopted the EPO's G 1/19 "any hardware" approach: a claim with technical means is an "invention" ([press summary](https://supremecourt.uk/cases/press-summary/uksc-2024-0131), [judgment](https://supremecourt.uk/uploads/uksc_2024_0131_judgment_1da6c10a83.pdf), both from supremecourt.uk; summary verified). It added an "intermediate step" that filters out features which do not contribute to technical character before novelty and inventive step [100] to [107], and remitted the case to the IPO.
- **IPO practice.** The IPO's practice notice of 14 July 2026 tells examiners to object under section 1(2) only if a claim has no technical means, then identify features that contribute to technical character, then assess novelty and inventive step on those features only (Pozzoli still applies). It does not say patenting becomes easier ([GOV.UK notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/search-and-examination-of-uk-patent-applications-under-the-patents-act-1977-as-amended/search-and-examination-of-uk-patent-applications-under-the-patents-act-1977-as-amended), verified). The Manual of Patent Practice section 1 is still being updated; examiners were told not to follow MoPP 1.07 to 1.51.7 meanwhile (search only, [Scottish Legal News](https://www.scottishlegal.com/articles/ukipo-updates-patent-examination-guidance-after-supreme-court-ai-ruling); MoPP: [section 1](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/manual-of-patent-practice-mopp/section-1-patentability)).
- **What that means for Pulse.** Pulse's novelty is in business rules and statistics: forecasting attendance, shrinkage multipliers, pricing thresholds, staffing ratios. Those are exactly the features the intermediate step removes. The EPO decision the Supreme Court discussed, T 154/04 *Duns Licensing*, concerned "estimating sales activity" and held that a business research method solves no technical problem; the only technical feature, using a processor, was obvious ([EPO OJ 2008, 46](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/official-journal/2008/02/p46), search only). GOV.UK still lists "a way of doing business" and "a mathematical method" as unpatentable ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/patent-your-invention/what-you-can-patent), verified). Emotional Perception's own application was refused again on 1 October 2026 (BL O/0894/26) for lack of inventive step, because "selecting a semantically better file" was not a technical result ([Brabners](https://www.brabners.com/insights/intellectual-property/emotional-perception-ukipo-refuses-ai-patent-application-again-this-time-for-lack-of-inventive-step), [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/thriller-at-the-ukipo-emotional-perception-and-the-ghost-of-aerotel-102o5l7), search only).
- **Cost and time.** IPO fees are at least £405 if you complete filing, search and examination (£75 filing online); a patent attorney "can cost several thousand pounds"; grant takes about five years; renewals yearly from year five ([GOV.UK apply for a patent](https://www.gov.uk/patent-your-invention), search only; fee rises from 1 April 2026 reported by [Hindles](https://www.hindles.co.uk/content/fee-increases-at-the-ukipo-from-1-april-2026), search only). Our estimate (not sourced): £4,000 to £10,000 to a granted UK patent with an attorney, far more for PCT and other countries, plus the cost of enforcing it.
- **Hidden costs.** Applications are published at 18 months, which hands the method to competitors whether or not a patent is granted. Novelty requires that the invention has not been "made publicly available anywhere in the world" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/patent-your-invention/what-you-can-patent), verified), and the method in `docs/pulse.md` is already on a public GitHub branch.
- **Verdict:** not realistic for the forecasting and decision logic. Possibly worth a 30-minute attorney chat only if we build something with a real technical effect (for example a method that measurably cuts server load or network traffic). Keep dated design notes anyway: they help prove authorship and support the section 64 prior-use defence, which lets a business keep doing what it was doing in good faith before someone else's priority date ([Patents Act 1977 s.64](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/37/section/64), verified).

### 4.2 Trade secrets

- **Definition.** Information is a trade secret if it is not "generally known among, or readily accessible to" people in the field, "has commercial value because it is secret", and "has been subject to reasonable steps ... to keep it secret" ([Trade Secrets (Enforcement, etc.) Regulations 2018, reg. 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/597/regulation/2), verified). Remedies include injunctions and damages; nothing to register; lasts as long as the secret does.
- **Fit.** Good fit for the constants learned from data, the prior tables, thresholds, backtest results, and any cross-operator priors. Poor fit for anything a customer can see on screen.
- **The problem today.** `docs/pulse.md` (method, constants, thresholds) and the code in `backend/src/intelligence` are on the public `claude/pulse` branch of a public repository with no licence file. That fails "readily accessible" and "reasonable steps" for everything published there. The repository having no LICENSE means others have no copyright licence to reuse the code (beyond what GitHub's terms allow on GitHub), but they can read and reimplement the ideas.

### 4.3 Copyright in code

- Code and "preparatory design material for a computer program" are literary works ([CDPA 1988 s.3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/3), verified). Protection is automatic and lasts life plus 70 years.
- It protects expression, not function. In *SAS Institute v World Programming* (CJEU C-406/10, 2012) the functionality, programming language and data formats were not protected; copying a manual's wording could infringe ([SCL report](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/), search only). So copyright stops someone lifting our code, not someone rebuilding Pulse from `docs/pulse.md`.
- Ownership: employees' work belongs to the employer "subject to any agreement to the contrary" ([CDPA s.11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/11), verified). Contractors and founders working before incorporation own their own copyright unless they assign it in writing.

### 4.4 Database right

- Exists where "there has been a substantial investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting the contents of the database" ([Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997, reg. 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/3032/regulation/13), verified).
- Weak for pooled booking data: the CJEU in *British Horseracing Board v William Hill* (C-203/02, 2004) held investment in *creating* data does not count ([CJEU press release](https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp04/aff/cp040089en.pdf), search only; [Court of Appeal 2005](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2005/863), search only). Booking records are created by the operators' sales, so the pool itself may not attract the right. Our curated reference sets (compiled legs, decay rates, holiday tables, verified interest mappings) have a better claim, because we invested in obtaining and verifying them.
- Post-Brexit qualification rules for UK database right were not checked (open question).
- In practice, contract terms protect a data pool better than database right.

### 4.5 Trade marks

- Cheap and certain. UK online application: £205 for one class, £60 for each extra class, from 1 April 2026 ([CITMA](https://www.citma.org.uk/resources/the-countdown-to-new-uk-ipo-fees-mb26.html), search only; confirm on GOV.UK). Likely classes: 9 (software), 42 (SaaS), possibly 35 (business analytics).
- "Pulse" alone is a common word in software and analytics and is likely crowded; "Seatfold" and "Seatfold Pulse" are more distinctive. We did not run a clearance search (open question).

### 4.6 Contracts and SaaS licensing structures

What vendors in this market do:

- **Licence, not sale, of a hosted service.** All the analytics vendors above deliver through hosted dashboards and APIs. None ships its models to the customer.
- **Aggregate data clauses.** Spektrix's earlier Data Processing Addendum (v1) let Spektrix collect anonymous or aggregated usage data where no individual can be identified, and said Spektrix "shall own all right, title and interest in and to the Aggregate Data" ([Spektrix DPA v1](https://support.spektrix.com/hc/article_attachments/360004268212), search only; whether v5 of January 2026 keeps it is unverified). Movio's processing terms let it "combine the Client Personal Data with Personal Data provided by other sources" for Movio Media and Madex (search only; the page now returns 404).
- **Free or cheap benchmark in exchange for data.** TRG Arts' benchmark is free to participants who connect a daily feed; data is "anonymised and aggregated" and "Your sales figures and customer data will not be identifiable" ([TRG Arts](https://trgarts.com/en-gb/benchmark), verified).
- UK commentary warns these clauses move valuable insight to the vendor and need careful definitions ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/saas-customer-data-ownership/), [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/contracts-and-commercial-law/1669278/data-ownership-in-saas-contracts-who-really-controls-the-clients-data), search only).

### 4.7 Ranked recommendation for a small company with no budget

1. **Stop publishing the method (this week, cost nil).** Make the repository private, or move `docs/pulse.md` and `backend/src/intelligence` to a private repository. Accept that what has already been public cannot be a trade secret, and aim secrecy at what comes next: fitted constants, cross-operator priors, backtest results, new modules. Check whether anything was mirrored or forked (open question).
2. **Keep all Pulse computation server-side (already true).** Clients and the admin portal receive forecasts, ranges and drivers, never parameters or code. Keep drivers in plain English and rounded, so the UI explains without exposing the full model. Rate-limit and log Pulse APIs so systematic extraction is visible.
3. **Write down "reasonable steps" (cost nil).** A one-page trade secrets register (what is secret, who can see it, where it lives), access control on the private repo and production data, confidentiality clauses for staff and contractors, and NDAs before any demo that goes beyond the UI. These steps are what the 2018 Regulations' definition looks for.
4. **Fix ownership (cost nil to low).** Written IP assignment from every founder and contractor who has written Pulse code or docs to the company. Without it, copyright stays with the individual (CDPA s.11 only covers employees).
5. **Customer terms (low cost, template plus one solicitor review).** SaaS licence with no reverse engineering, no use of outputs to build a competing model, no scraping, confidentiality of the service; a clear "aggregated data" clause that matches the data-pool design in section 5; a UK GDPR Article 28 data processing agreement.
6. **Dated records (cost nil).** Keep the git history and a dated design log for each Pulse module (what, when, who). Useful for proving authorship (copyright), for the s.64 prior-use defence, and as evidence if a former staff member or partner takes the work.
7. **Trade marks (about £265 to £325 in official fees).** File "SEATFOLD" in classes 9 and 42 first; add "SEATFOLD PULSE" when budget allows, after a clearance search.
8. **Patent: not now.** Revisit only if we build something with a measurable technical effect, and then only after a free initial attorney consultation. Never publish a design we might patent before filing.

The durable protection is not legal: it is the data network (section 5), integration with the booking system, and published accuracy that competitors do not match.

That is the moat, and it is stronger than any patent available to us.

### 5.2 UK GDPR and ICO position

Roles:

- For bookings, Seatfold is the operator's **processor**. The ICO: "If you act outside your instructions or process for your own purposes", "you will step outside your role as a processor and become a controller for that processing" ([ICO, what does it mean if you are a processor](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/controllers-and-processors/what-does-it-mean-if-you-are-a-processor/), verified).
- On AI specifically, the ICO says that if an AI supplier uses shared data to train its own model without the original organisation's involvement, "the AI service provider would be a separate controller", and that a supplier "would be the controller for any information they receive to help train their AI model" ([ICO innovation advice, previously asked questions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/innovation-advice/previously-asked-questions/), verified). It has rejected the argument that a developer stays a processor because clients benefit from model improvements ([ICO, allocating controllership across the generative AI supply chain](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-work-on-artificial-intelligence/response-to-the-consultation-series-on-generative-ai/allocating-controllership-across-the-generative-ai-supply-chain/), search only).
- Contract labels do not decide this; the facts do ([ICO, how do you determine whether you are a controller or processor](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/controllers-and-processors/controllers-and-processors/how-do-you-determine-whether-you-are-a-controller-or-processor), search only).

Anonymisation:

- ICO anonymisation guidance (published 28 March 2025): identifiability is a spectrum; the test is whether identification is "reasonably likely", using a "motivated intruder" test; risk need only be remote, not zero ([ICO, about this guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/about-this-guidance/), search only).
- Status depends on whose hands the data is in: the same data can be personal for the holder and anonymous for a recipient who cannot reasonably re-identify, but for joint controllers and processors the holder's status governs ([ICO, how do we ensure anonymisation is effective](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/how-do-we-ensure-anonymisation-is-effective/), verified). Because Seatfold holds the raw bookings as processor, we cannot argue the pool is anonymous merely because it is anonymous to other operators. The pooled data has to be non-identifying in our own hands too.
- The same page cites the NHS small-numbers standard (k-anonymity of five) and an example of statistics being withheld when counts got small (verified).
- The EU Court of Justice (*EDPS v SRB*, C-413/23 P, 4 September 2025) took a similar recipient-relative view of pseudonymised data. Not binding in the UK but persuasive ([Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/eu-the-srb-decision-a-new-era-for-personal-data-and-data-processing-agreements), search only).

Lawful basis:

- Operators need a lawful basis for sharing customer data with a supplier for model improvement, should anonymise where possible, and must assess whether a new use is compatible with the original purpose ([ICO previously asked questions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/innovation-advice/previously-asked-questions/), verified).
- Legitimate interests with a documented three-part assessment is the realistic basis; the ICO updated its legitimate interests guidance on 23 March 2026 for the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 ([ICO legitimate interests](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/), verified). The Act's new "recognised legitimate interests" basis (in force 5 February 2026) covers listed public-interest purposes and does not fit model training ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/02/uk-commencement-of-the-data-protection-provisions-in-the-data-use-and-access-act/), search only; the list itself was not checked).

Competition law (not GDPR, but it bites on pools of competitors' data):

- Under UK and EU horizontal guidance, a platform can act as a "hub" for information exchange between competitors; aggregated data that cannot be attributed to a particular business is lower risk; participants should see only their own data and the final aggregate ([Linklaters on CMA draft guidance](https://linklaters.com/insights/blogs/linkingcompetition/2023/march/new-guidance-in-the-uk-for-horizontal-agreements_but-pitfalls-remain), [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-hk/knowledge/publications/7debe299/the-new-horizontal-guidelines-information-exchange), search only; final CMA text not checked). Two cinemas in the same town are competitors. Never let pooled data reveal another operator's prices, current sales or future programme.

### 5.3 A design that should work (to confirm with a solicitor)

1. **Pool only show-level, non-personal facts.** From each operator's `ShowOutcome` rows: slot, weekday, release week band, genre, format, capacity band, admissions share, booking-curve points. No customer ids, no baskets, no loyalty data, no prices. Loyalty (RFM), baskets and recommendations stay strictly per tenant.
2. **Make aggregation an instructed processing step.** The DPA lists "produce anonymised, aggregated show statistics" as part of the service the operator instructs. The ICO's processor test turns on purposes; the operator gets the benefit (better priors), and we say so in the contract.
3. **Treat the cross-operator model as ours, as controller of nothing personal.** Fit pooled priors only from the aggregates. If any pooled input were personal, we would be a controller for that training and need our own lawful basis and transparency, which is the Movio Digital Audience Service pattern (legitimate interests, privacy notice).
4. **Suppress small cells.** Drop or merge any cell with fewer than five shows from at least three operators (our choice, informed by the ICO's k=5 example), and never publish a figure attributable to one cinema.
5. **Consent at business level, opt-in.** Each operator opts in to "Pulse network" in the contract (commercial consent, not GDPR consent), can leave, and gets their own data plus network priors only.
6. **Transparency.** Operators add a line to their privacy notice that booking data is used, in aggregated form, to improve forecasting. Seatfold publishes a short note describing the pool.
7. **Competition safeguards.** Regional cells wide enough that one competitor cannot be inferred; no prices or forward-looking data; minimum participant counts; written policy.
